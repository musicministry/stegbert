Weddings at St. Egbert’s

Guidance for liturgical music selection

Congratulations on your big day! We offer many prayers for you for a lifetime of blessings and happiness.

For Catholics, a wedding is a liturgical celebration of the sacrament of Holy Matrimony (marriage), a sacrifice of two people becoming one in God. What is liturgy, you ask? Liturgy is at the heart of what the church does: it is our public worship of God, our creator. And what is worship? It is giving thanks and honor to God that is due to him for all he has given to us. It is our expression of honor, acknowledgement, and respect to and for God because he is greater than us in all ways.

Everything about the wedding liturgy, including the music, is about your worship of God and God alone. For this reason, the Church prohibits popular music of any kind within the liturgy. Liturgical music must be deliberate, appropriate, and “correspond to the spirit of the liturgical celebration itself” (Instruction on Music in the Liturgy, 9). To help you plan your worship of God at your wedding liturgy, we provide instructions and suitable music options below. Click on the title of any piece to listen to a sample recording.

We encourage you to use this web form to submit your choices. For questions or assistance, or if you would prefer to have us select your music for you, please reach out to Matt Grossi, music director and organist, at mgrossi@stegbert.org.

Processional

The entrance procession involves the couple’s families (parents), wedding party, and the bride herself. You have a few options:

  1. Sung processional hymn: Like at Mass, the hymn would be sung during the entire processional. Choose one from the list of processional hymns below.
    OR
  2. One instrumental piece: Many couples opt for a single piece as a musical portrayal of familial unity. Pachelbel’s Canon in D is a popular choice, with the slower movements played during the seating of families and bridal party and the faster climatic movement summoning the bride, but you may choose any one piece from the instrumental options below.
    OR
  3. One instrumental piece for the seating of the families and the bridal party and a second piece for the bridal entrance. Choose the first one from the instrumental options and the second from either the instrumental options or the marches below.
